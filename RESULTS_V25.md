## The mean disagrees with the rank, again

Heartwood has the **highest mean** (79.8) and the **third-best rank** (3.62). Those cannot both be the headline.

Its mean advantage over the ridge is **+1.1** points. Excluding `uea:EthanolConcentration` alone — where it wins by +22.3 — that becomes **-0.3**.

So one dataset carries the whole advantage. `RESULTS_V24.md` flagged the same pattern; this quantifies it. **The rank is the honest summary.**

## What this means

**A linear shrinkage estimator is the right head, and MiniROCKET chose correctly.** The two best-ranked models on this bank are both linear, and *every* nonlinear head — random forest, extremely randomised trees, histogram boosting, k-NN, an MLP — ranks below them. The bank's value is in its features; what sits on top should mostly get out of the way.

**The scouting note in `validation/DATALETS.md` was half right and is corrected here.** It observed that a tournament beat a plain histogram booster and speculated *"the answer might not be trees"*. The answer is indeed not trees — but not because something more elaborate wins. It is because something *simpler* does.

**Heartwood is competitive, not dominant.** Nothing beat it by the bar, and it beat nothing by the bar. On rank it sits third, behind two linear heads; on mean it leads, on the strength of one dataset. That is consistent with `RESULTS_V24.md` and neither better nor worse than it.

**The outcome §5 named as least welcome did not occur.** A stock histogram booster ranks 5.75, -8.3 points against the ridge — far from beating it or Heartwood. The temporal machinery is not worse than doing nothing special.
