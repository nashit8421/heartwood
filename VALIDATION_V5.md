# Validation Plan V5 — the conditional claim

**Status: PRE-REGISTERED. Written and committed before any V5 dataset was downloaded
or any V5 result computed.** Same discipline as `VALIDATION.md`, same reason: the natural
drift of this work is toward flattering results, and by now there is direct evidence of
that drift in this repository.

---

## 1. Why this exists

`VALIDATION.md` asked whether the core claim — Heartwood beats the aggregate workaround —
transfers to real data. It reported PASS. `validation/CORRECTION.md` shows that verdict
was an artifact: the aggregate baseline summarised windows with NaN-propagating NumPy
reductions, so on PhysioNet ICU (80% missing) one absent cell voided a whole statistic and
94% of the baseline's design matrix was empty. Heartwood's own `interval_stat` has always
skipped NaN. The benchmark was varying two things at once and crediting the difference to
the one under study.

With that fixed, **H1 fails**: 5/15 cells, against a pass bar of 60% and a fail bar of 50%.
Restated per dataset:

| dataset | regime | Heartwood − `agg` (fair baseline) |
|---|---|---|
| ICU | series summarise well | +0.7, +1.4, +0.3, +1.5, +0.5 — 0/5 |
| credit | series summarise well | −0.4, −0.0, +0.6, +0.8, −0.2 — 0/5 |
| HAR | series do not summarise well | +3.5, +5.1, +4.6 — wins |

That is not a random scatter. It is a clean split, and it suggests a claim that has never
been tested: **Heartwood helps exactly when a global summary demonstrably loses
information, and costs nothing when it does not.** The v0.3 claim was unconditional and is
dead. This document tests the conditional one.

**Every hypothesis below was generated by looking at the corrected v0.3 results.** That
makes those results *motivation*, not evidence. So V5 is evaluated **only on datasets not
used in v0.3**. The old ones are reported alongside, clearly marked, and excluded from
every verdict.

---

## 2. The regime diagnostic, fixed now

The conditional claim needs "aggregation demonstrably loses" to be decidable **without
running Heartwood**, otherwise the condition can be fitted to the answer. For a dataset
`d` at training size `n`, on that dataset's pre-registered headline metric:

```
aggregation_loss(d, n) = max(wagg4, wagg8, raw_flat, minirocket, minirocket10k) − agg
```

* `aggregation_loss ≥ +5 pt` → **shape regime** (something beyond global summaries pays)
* `aggregation_loss ≤ +2 pt` → **summary regime** (global summaries are enough)
* otherwise → **ambiguous**, reported in full but excluded from H-V5.1

Every term is a baseline. Heartwood never enters, so the classification cannot be tuned.
Applied to the v0.3 datasets it recovers the split above (HAR +12.8 → shape; ICU −1.1 and
credit −0.3 → summary), which is why it is worth using — but that is a sanity check on the
definition, not a result.

## 3. Datasets (locked)

**Arm A — the empty cell.** No v0.3 dataset had *both* a genuine static block *and*
shape-regime series: ICU and credit have static covariates whose series summarise away;
HAR has shape-regime series but its "static block" is a subject identifier that is
disjoint between train and test, so `static_only` sits at chance. The cell the library was
designed for was never occupied. Arm A occupies it.

| # | dataset | static | series | target | headline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **PTB-XL** (PhysioNet 1.0.3) | age, sex, height, weight — real per-patient demographics shipped as data | 12-lead ECG, 100 Hz, 1000 steps | 5 diagnostic superclasses | balanced accuracy |

PTB-XL qualifies under `VALIDATION.md` §3: real per-row statics, raw per-row series, a
per-row target, n ≈ 21,000, and public download without credentials. Every preprocessing
decision is fixed here, before the data is parsed:

* **Split** is the dataset's own `strat_fold` — folds 1–8 train, fold 10 test, fold 9
  unused. Not re-drawn. It is patient-disjoint by construction.
* **Label** is the diagnostic superclass (NORM, MI, STTC, CD, HYP) via `scp_statements.csv`.
  A record is kept only if its SCP codes map to **exactly one** superclass; records mapping
  to none and records mapping to two or more are dropped, and **both counts are reported**.
  Balanced accuracy over five classes needs one label per row.
* **Static block** is age, sex, height, weight. PTB-XL encodes ages over 89 as `300`; those
  become 90. Missing height and weight stay missing — no imputation, per protocol.
* **Series** is `records100`: 12 leads x 1000 samples (100 Hz x 10 s), held as float32
  because float64 would be 2 GB. The 500 Hz records are not used.
* **Size grid** is the standard n in {100, 250, 500, 1000, all}. The whole-split cell is
  ~17,000 rows of 12x1000 series and is expected to exceed a 30-minute-per-fit budget; if
  it does it is recorded as **infeasible with the reason**, exactly as EigenWorms was, and
  not quietly dropped.

**Arm B — regime robustness.** The UEA multivariate archive, **ranks 9–16 by training-set
size**, extending the v0.3 rule (which took ranks 1–8) mechanically to the next eight. Ties
broken alphabetically. Chosen by `n` alone, before any accuracy was looked at, and
verified against the archive rather than recalled:

Epilepsy (137), Handwriting (150), RacketSports (151), HandMovementDirection (160),
Libras (180), NATOPS (180), SelfRegulationSCP2 (200), Heartbeat (204).

No static block; `X_static=None`. This arm exists to spread H-V5.2 across many datasets
and both regimes cheaply.

A dataset that cannot be obtained, parsed, or fitted within budget is reported as
`unavailable` **with the reason**, exactly as EigenWorms was. It is not replaced.

## 4. Hypotheses — frozen, with numeric pass/fail

**H-V5.1 — the conditional core claim.** Two halves, both required.
* *Helps where it should*: on **shape-regime** cells, Heartwood beats `agg` by ≥ 2 points
  on **≥ 60%** of cells.
* *Harms nowhere*: on **summary-regime** cells, Heartwood is within 2 points of `agg` on
  **≥ 80%** of cells.

*Fails if* the shape-regime win rate is < 50%, **or** more than 20% of summary-regime cells
are worse than `agg` by > 2 points. Failure means the conditional claim is dead too, and
the honest conclusion is that Heartwood does not reliably beat aggregation even where
aggregation demonstrably loses.

**H-V5.2 — regime robustness.** Over all V5 cells, Heartwood's mean rank among
{heartwood, agg, wagg4, wagg8, raw_flat, best MiniROCKET} is **strictly better than every
individual baseline's** mean rank.
*Fails if* any single baseline matches or beats it. This is the "no baseline is safe
across regimes, but one model is" claim, and it is the strongest thing the corrected v0.3
data hints at.

**H-V5.3 — the empty cell.** On Arm A, Heartwood beats **both** `agg` **and** the better
MiniROCKET by ≥ 2 points at a **majority of training sizes**.
*Fails if* it beats neither at a majority of sizes.
Precondition, reported either way: Arm A's static block must be non-degenerate —
`static_only` ahead of the majority-class rate by ≥ 2 points. If it is degenerate, A1 is a
temporal-only dataset in disguise, H-V5.3 is void, and that is recorded as a failure to
occupy the cell rather than quietly reinterpreted.
This is the hypothesis most likely to fail: on HAR, the one shape-regime dataset so far,
MiniROCKET beat Heartwood by 5–9 points. If PTB-XL behaves like HAR, this fails and the
library has no demonstrated unique niche.

## 5. Protocol

Unchanged from `VALIDATION.md` §5 except where the correction changed it.

* Heartwood on **library defaults**. Baselines get matched rounds/depth/learning rate.
  No tuning, anywhere, for anyone. Any tuned run is a separate, labelled, non-headline arm.
* Small-data curve n ∈ {100, 250, 500, 1000, all}, **5 subsample seeds** — the collapse to
  one seed on official-split datasets is fixed and is part of what V5 exists to avoid
  repeating. The whole-training-split cell of an official split gets 2 seeds, since every
  seed there sees identical rows and only re-measures the learner's own randomness.
* MiniROCKET runs at **both** 2,000 and 10,000 kernels and is credited with the better —
  neither budget dominates, and a baseline should not lose by being under-run.
* Aggregate baselines are NaN-skipping. `agg_naive`/`wagg8_naive` are carried alongside so
  every table shows what that choice is worth.
* Missing data stays missing. Headline metric fixed per dataset here, before running.
* Splits fixed by seed, never redrawn after seeing a result.

## 6. Rules

Identical in force to `VALIDATION.md` §2: no tuning, no dataset dropping, no hypothesis
edits after seeing results, report every dataset × every baseline, one headline metric per
dataset fixed in advance, splits never redrawn. Post-hoc observations are allowed and must
be labelled "exploratory, not pre-registered".

**One rule added, because of what the correction found.** Any baseline must handle missing
data the same way Heartwood does, and a test enforces it
(`test_baseline_and_heartwood_agree_on_what_a_summary_of_gaps_means`). A benchmark that
varies two things at once is not a benchmark, and this repository has now produced one.

**Algorithm changes are out of scope for V5.** If V5 motivates a change to the library —
better candidate targeting is the standing open problem — that change is proposed, then
pre-registered as V6 and evaluated against these same thresholds. Tuning the algorithm
against V5 results and then reporting V5 would be the same mistake in a new costume.

## 7. Threats to validity

* **One dataset in Arm A.** Whatever H-V5.3 says, it says it about PTB-XL. A single
  dataset cannot establish that the empty cell is generally winnable, only that it is or
  is not winnable here.
* **PTB-XL preprocessing is a degree of freedom**: 100 Hz over 500 Hz, superclass over
  the finer label sets, the fold assignment, dropping unlabelled records. All fixed in
  code, committed, and never revised after seeing a score.
* **The regime diagnostic could be wrong**, in the sense of not carving nature at the
  joints. It is a definition, fixed in advance; if it classifies datasets in a way that
  makes H-V5.1 vacuous — e.g. if every V5 dataset lands in one regime — that is reported
  as an uninformative result rather than repaired.
* **ECG is not a random draw from "real data".** Neither was ICU. Three regimes' worth of
  medical and sensor data is still not a survey, and the conclusion is about these
  datasets.

## 8. What counts as done

V5 answers one question: *does Heartwood have a regime where it is the right tool, and can
that regime be identified in advance?* PASS on H-V5.1 and H-V5.3 means yes and the README
says so, narrowly. FAIL on both means the library is a well-engineered research artifact
without a demonstrated niche, and the README says **that**, in the same prominence as
anything else. Either sentence is a result. Only an unwritten one is a failure.
